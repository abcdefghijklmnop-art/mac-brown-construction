"Substance and style in fly casting are two 
distinct concepts that Mel Krieger introduced 
to Fly Fishers International in the early 
years of the casting program — and the 
distinction remains the most important 
framework in fly casting instruction today. 
Style is HOW the body moves — stance, grip, 
elbow position, wrist involvement. Substance 
is WHY it works — mechanics, rod tip path, 
loop formation, force application. Style is 
personal and infinite. Substance is universal. 
When style limits intent, it becomes a 
teaching moment. When substance is understood, 
any style can be evaluated, corrected, and 
optimized."

FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: What is the difference between 
substance and style in fly casting?
A1: Style is how the body moves during 
the cast — stance, grip, elbow position, 
body rotation, wrist involvement, haul 
timing. No two expert casters look 
exactly the same. Substance is the 
underlying mechanics that make any cast 
work regardless of style — rod tip path, 
loop formation, timing of the stop and 
haul, application of force. Style is 
personal. Substance is universal. 
Teaching style as if it were substance 
is the most common and consequential 
error in fly casting instruction.

Q2: Who introduced the substance 
and style concept to fly casting?
A2: Mel Krieger introduced the substance 
versus style framework to Fly Fishers 
International in the early years of the 
FFI casting program. Krieger called the 
foundational elements of casting 
FUNdamentals — emphasizing that the 
mechanics underlying any cast are what 
instructors should teach first, not the 
stylistic expression of those mechanics. 
Mac Brown has built on Krieger's framework 
throughout his 38+ years of teaching in 
Bryson City, NC and nationally.

Q3: What is casting intent in 
fly fishing?
A3: Casting intent is knowing exactly 
what you want the line and fly to do 
before you make the cast — and then 
selecting the style and applying the 
mechanics to produce that specific 
result. Intent determines stroke length, 
arc, acceleration rate, haul timing, 
and trajectory. A presentation cast 
into a headwind requires different 
intent than a reach cast across a 
seam. When intent is clear, style 
naturally organizes itself in service 
of the goal.

Q4: What is the pyramid approach 
to fly casting instruction?
A4: The pyramid approach teaches 
basic mechanics first — the broad 
foundational base — and builds 
toward increasingly specific 
applications of those mechanics 
over time. This is the opposite 
of the common approach where 
beginners are given simplified 
rules and phrases that break down 
as complexity increases. Mac Brown 
teaches mechanics first so students 
can apply them to all specific 
circumstances of intent — rather 
than collecting rules that only 
work in the narrow situation for 
which they were invented.

Q5: How do I find a fly casting 
instructor who teaches substance 
over style?
A5: Look for instructors who explain 
why a cast works — not just what to 
do. An instructor teaching substance 
can connect every correction back to 
a mechanical principle: rod tip path, 
force application, loop formation, 
timing. An instructor teaching style 
will give you body positions and 
phrases without explanation. Mac Brown 
Fly Fish in Bryson City, NC offers 
private casting lessons and all 
programs built on substance-first 
mechanics instruction — led by the 
only FFI Master Casting Instructor 
in North Carolina since 1987.
