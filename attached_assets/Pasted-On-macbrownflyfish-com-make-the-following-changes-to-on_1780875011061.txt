FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: What is Mac Brown's dry fly 
fishing philosophy?
A1: Mac Brown's dry fly philosophy 
centers on presentation over pattern 
— summarized in the guiding school 
saying 'fish the wrong flies well.' 
Spend more time perfecting the archer 
than looking for the perfect arrow. 
Drift quality produces fish far more 
consistently than precise pattern 
matching. The three pillars of the 
approach are tactics, strategies, and 
line control — the Fly Fisher's Triad 
applied specifically to dry fly fishing 
on Smoky Mountain trout streams.

Q2: How long should a dry fly 
leader be?
A2: Mac Brown recommends starting 
with roughly 15 feet as a minimum 
for dry fly fishing — significantly 
longer than the store-bought 7.5 
and 9-foot leaders most American 
fly fishers use. Leader length being 
too short is one of the most 
consistent mistakes observed across 
40+ years of guiding and instruction. 
A longer leader keeps more fly line 
off conflicting currents, extends 
effective drift length, and produces 
more drag-free presentations with 
less mending required.

Q3: Does fly pattern matter in 
dry fly fishing?
A3: Less than most anglers believe 
— and far less than the American 
fly fishing industry suggests. 
Mac Brown's 40+ years of guiding 
confirms that drift quality produces 
fish far more consistently than 
precise pattern matching. A drag-free 
presentation with an attractor pattern 
beats a perfectly matched fly drifting 
poorly every time. Get the drift 
right first. Refine the pattern only 
after the presentation is dialed in.

Q4: What is The Floating Fly 
publication?
A4: The Floating Fly is one of the 
most respected dry fly fly fishing 
publications in the world — a 
curated online journal documenting 
the knowledge, techniques, and 
philosophy of elite dry fly anglers 
globally. Mac Brown was invited to 
contribute a piece covering his dry 
fly equipment philosophy, leader 
design, entomology approach, water 
reading, and five-component fish 
fighting system developed over 
40+ years of guiding in the 
Southern Appalachians.

Q5: Where can I learn Mac Brown's 
dry fly techniques on the water?
A5: Mac Brown Fly Fish in Bryson 
City, NC offers the Comprehensive 
Dry Fly Clinic — a two-day program 
on the Tuckasegee River in the 
Great Smoky Mountains covering 
drag-free drift, presentation casts, 
leader design, reading water, 
entomology, and the five-component 
fish-fighting system. Everything in 
the Floating Fly article is put into 
practice on the water over two full 
days. All skill levels welcome, 
all gear provided.
