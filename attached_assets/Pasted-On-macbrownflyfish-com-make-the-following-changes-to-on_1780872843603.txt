FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: What is the best time of year 
for fly fishing in the Smoky Mountains?
A1: Spring and fall are the strongest 
seasons. Spring — March through May — 
brings the most diverse hatches of the 
year: Quill Gordons, Blue Quills, March 
Browns, Hendricksons, and Black Caddis 
on the Tuckasegee. Water temperatures 
sit in the prime 45-65°F range and 
trout are most actively feeding. Fall 
— September through November — brings 
the October Caddis hatch, aggressive 
brown trout feeding behavior, and the 
most spectacular scenery of the year. 
Both seasons produce triple-digit fish 
counts on guided float trips.

Q2: Is fly fishing good in summer 
in the Smoky Mountains?
A2: Summer fishing drops significantly 
on lower elevation rivers as water 
temperatures climb into the 70s during 
June and July. Stop fishing for trout 
at 65°F — above this temperature trout 
are physiologically stressed and 
mortality risk increases significantly. 
In summer fish early morning before 
temperatures rise, target higher 
elevation wild streams in the park 
where water stays cooler, and always 
carry a thermometer. The wild streams 
of Great Smoky Mountains National Park 
remain fishable throughout summer at 
elevation.

Q3: Is fly fishing good in winter 
in the Smoky Mountains?
A3: Yes — and it is significantly 
underrated. The Tuckasegee River 
delayed harvest section and the 
Nantahala River tailwater both fish 
well all winter. Midge and BWO hatches 
occur on mild overcast afternoons. 
Fish are less active but concentrated 
in predictable slow deep lies. The 
biggest advantage of winter fishing 
is solitude — world-class trout water 
with almost no other anglers on it.

Q4: When does the delayed harvest 
season run in North Carolina?
A4: North Carolina delayed harvest 
sections — including the Tuckasegee 
River through downtown Bryson City — 
run October 1 through May 31 each 
year. During delayed harvest, catch 
and release is required and no harvest 
is permitted. The section is heavily 
stocked throughout the season and 
provides some of the most accessible 
and productive trout fishing in 
Western North Carolina regardless 
of skill level.

Q5: What water temperature is best 
for trout fishing in the Smokies?
A5: The prime range is 45-65°F. 
Below 45°F fish are lethargic and 
takes are subtle — fish slowly with 
small nymphs in deep slow water. 
From 45-55°F hatches begin and dry 
fly action becomes viable. From 
55-65°F all techniques fire and 
the most diverse hatches of the 
year occur. At 65°F stop fishing 
for trout entirely — switch to 
warm water species or target higher 
elevation streams. Always verify 
with a thermometer before heading 
out — check the live conditions 
widget at macbrownflyfish.com for 
current Tuckasegee and Nantahala 
water temperatures.
