Q1: What is the best streamer fly

patterns for brown trout on Western 
North Carolina rivers are Gary Borger's 
Bullhead Sculpin — a simple, acoustically 
effective sculpin imitation that produced 
six browns over six pounds in a single 
day on the lower Nantahala — Rich 
Brostic's two-material olive streamer 
(black chenille body, olive marabou 
wing as long as the hook shank — 
ties in under a minute), and Mike 
Sexton's Blank Saver. One- to three-
inch streamers have consistently 
outproduced larger articulated patterns 
on Smoky Mountain rivers over 40+ 
years of guided trips.

Q2: What retrieval rate works best 
for streamer fishing?
A2: Vary the rate constantly — the 
most common streamer fishing mistake 
is using the same retrieval speed 
throughout the drift. Slow strips, 
fast strips, pauses, and erratic 
combinations all trigger different 
responses from fish on different days. 
Most strikes on streamers come during 
the pause or on the first strip after 
a pause. Mending the fly line during 
the swing changes the direction and 
speed of the streamer through 
differential currents — often triggering 
strikes when a straight swing produces 
nothing. It is more about your 
technique than the fly.

Q3: What fly line is best for 
streamer fishing?
A3: Matching line density to where 
the fish are holding is critical — 
one of the most common mistakes is 
using a floating line when targeting 
big fish that hold near the bottom 
in big water. Build or purchase high-
density sink tip and full sink 
configurations that get the fly down 
into the productive zone. For bank 
fishing and shallower swings a 
floating line with a weighted fly 
works well. For deep runs and 
tailwaters on the Nantahala a 
moderate to fast sink tip puts 
the fly in front of the fish.

Q4: Is streamer fishing good for 
beginners and children?
A4: Yes — streamer fishing is one 
of the best introductory techniques 
in fly fishing because the fly line 
remains under tension during the 
swing. When a fish strikes it is 
typically hooked without requiring 
a precise hook set. Both of Mac 
Brown's children had consistent 
action swinging streamers at very 
young ages. The technique covers 
vast amounts of water efficiently, 
requires minimal casting skill for 
basic swing presentations, and 
produces fish across all skill levels.

Q5: Where can I learn advanced 
streamer techniques and line control 
with Gary Borger and Mac Brown?
A5: Mac Brown and Gary Borger teach 
the all-day Advanced Line Control 
class together annually at The Fly

retrieval variation, and advanced 
fly fishing technique. Check the 
Events Calendar at 
macbrownflyfish.com/events-calendar 
for upcoming show dates and locations. 
Guided streamer trips on the Nantahala 
River and Tuckasegee River are 
available year-round through Mac 
Brown Fly Fish in Bryson City, NC.
