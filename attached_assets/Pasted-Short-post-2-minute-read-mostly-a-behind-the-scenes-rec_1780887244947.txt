Q1: Is the Nantahala River good 
for streamer fishing?
A1: Yes — the Nantahala River tailwater 
holds large brown trout that respond 
aggressively to streamers, particularly 
in fall when dropping water temperatures 
trigger predatory feeding behavior from 
the biggest fish in the system. The 
Nantahala's consistent cold water 
temperature — pulling from the bottom 
of Lake Nantahala year-round — keeps 
brown trout healthy and large. Mac Brown 
has filmed television episodes on the 
Nantahala with Kelly Galloup documenting 
exactly what it takes to consistently 
land large brown trout on streamers.

Q2: What conditions affect streamer 
fishing success?
A2: Streamer fishing success is heavily 
condition-dependent. The key variables 
to document in a fishing journal are 
wind direction and speed, barometric 
pressure and trend, water temperature, 
light levels, water clarity, moon 
phases, and insect hatch activity. 
Cold fronts moving through — bringing 
northwest winds, falling barometric 
pressure, and cooler drier air — 
can shut streamer fishing down 
temporarily before it turns on. 
A full moon combined with a cold 
front creates challenging conditions. 
Keep a detailed journal to identify 
the condition patterns that produce 
fish on your home water.

Q3: How do you vary streamer patterns 
on the Nantahala River?
A3: Kelly Galloup's approach — 
documented during the FFTV filming 
on the Nantahala — involves switching 
patterns throughout the day as part 
of on-the-water research. Pattern 
trigger mechanisms include color, 
size, profile, action, and acoustic 
footprint. Different patterns trigger 
different responses from fish at 
different times of day and in 
different water conditions. The key 
is systematic variation rather than 
random fly changes — changing one 
variable at a time and noting 
which change produces the response.

Q4: What gear does Kelly Galloup 
recommend for streamer fishing?
A4: Kelly Galloup worked with 
Scientific Anglers to develop a 
200-grain streamer line specifically 
designed for throwing heavy articulated 
patterns — and developed a dedicated 
streamer rod with St. Croix capable 
of handling the demands of throwing 
serious weight all day. Purpose-built 
streamer gear reduces fatigue and 
improves accuracy when casting 
heavy flies at distance. Mac Brown 
recommends matching line weight 
and sink rate to water depth and 
current speed on the Nantahala.

Q5: Can I book a guided streamer 
trip on the Nantahala River?
A5: Yes — Mac Brown Fly Fish offers 
guided float trips and wade trips on 
the Nantahala River year-round. Fall

trout are among the most exciting 
guided days of the entire year.
