Q1: What is the Fly Fishers Triad?
A1: The Fly Fishers Triad is a methodology 
for fly fishing development built on three 
equally important sides — strategy, line 
control, and tactics. In an ideal world 
they form an equilateral triangle. In 
reality every angler's triangle is a 
different shape reflecting where their 
strengths and gaps lie. The Triad gives 
anglers and instructors a shared language 
for identifying exactly what needs to 
improve and in what order — rather than 
buying more gear or changing flies.

Q2: What is strategy in fly fishing?
A2: Strategy is the combination of 
knowledge, wisdom, and intent applied 
to how you approach, read, and cover 
water. It determines whether you camp 
on a honey hole or rest it wisely, 
whether you grid the water lane by 
layer or chase random rises. Strategy 
develops most slowly — it requires 
accumulated experience and deliberate 
attention to stream detail over many 
seasons. Fly fishers with age on them 
typically have the best strategies 
because they have earned the most 
failures and lessons.

Q3: What is line control in the 
Fly Fishers Triad?
A3: Line control is the kinesthetic 
side of the Triad — every cast, mend, 
retrieve, and drift correction. It is 
the physical act of repositioning your 
fly to achieve a specific intent. The 
Triad of line control breaks into 
pause, power, and path — the three 
elements that govern all casting and 
all mending. Line control is the 
fastest of the three sides to improve 
with proper instruction — a good coach 
can identify and correct faults that 
self-guided practice reinforces for 
years.

Q4: What are tactics in fly fishing?
A4: Tactics are the subtle techniques 
applied during the drift — the how 
of presenting the fly moment to moment. 
The Triad of tactics breaks into active 
(movement with purpose), passive (dead 
drift at the speed of the current), 
and erratic (unpredictable, triggering 
reaction strikes). Most drifts involve 
all three in sequence. A nymph fished 
down to a likely lie (passive), lifted 
at the end of the drift (active), then 
jigged twice before the pickup (erratic) 
is a complete tactical presentation.

Q5: How does the Fly Fishers Triad 
apply to guided trips and instruction?
A5: The Triad is the diagnostic framework 
Mac Brown uses on every guided trip and 
every instructional program — identifying 
precisely which side of the triangle is 
limiting a client's success and addressing 
it directly. Most self-taught fly fishers 
have a severely underdeveloped strategy 
side and a fault-ridden line control side 
— and spend their time and money on 
tactics and gear instead. The Triad 
taught at Mac Brown Fly Fish in Bryson 
City, NC has been the foundation of the 
Fly Fishing Guide School curriculum 
for 35+ years.
