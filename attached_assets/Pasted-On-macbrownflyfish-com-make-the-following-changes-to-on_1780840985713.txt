FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: When does the Black Caddis hatch 
on the Tuckasegee River?
A1: The Black Caddis hatch on the 
Tuckasegee River delayed harvest section 
typically begins on warm days in early 
March — when afternoon temperatures 
climb enough to trigger the first 
significant surface activity of the 
season. The hatch runs through April 
and is most productive on overcast 
mild days when the insects emerge 
steadily rather than in short bursts. 
The delayed harvest section flows 
right through downtown Bryson City 
making it one of the most accessible 
early season hatches in Western NC.

Q2: What flies work best for the 
Black Caddis hatch on the Tuckasegee?
A2: Fish a three-fly rig focused on 
the film. Mac Brown's preferred setup: 
Charlie Craven's Clown Caddis on the 
point for high visibility and film 
riding, Gary LaFontaine's Sparkle 
Emergent Pupa as the first dropper, 
and an attractor emerger pattern as 
the second dropper. Treat the dry fly 
with Frogs Fanny or Dry Magic for 
all-day floatation. The emerger 
patterns should ride in or just below 
the surface film — not deep in the 
water column.

Q3: What is the best technique for 
fishing the Black Caddis hatch?
A3: Fish the film — not the surface 
and not deep. The most productive 
approach during the Black Caddis hatch 
is a three-fly rig with the best 
floating pattern on the point and low-
riding emergers as droppers. Cover 
current lanes methodically — target 
the foam lines and seams where emerging 
insects concentrate. Swinging attractor 
wet flies through rising fish is also 
highly productive when fish are actively 
feeding near the surface.

Q4: Is the Tuckasegee River good for 
fly fishing in early spring?
A4: Yes — the Tuckasegee River delayed 
harvest section through Bryson City is 
one of the best early spring fly fishing 
destinations in the eastern United States. 
The delayed harvest program runs October 
through May, keeping the river stocked 
and well-managed through the prime spring 
hatch season. Black Caddis, March Browns, 
Hendricksons, and Quill Gordons all emerge 
on the Tuckasegee in March and April — 
often producing simultaneous mixed hatches 
that challenge even experienced anglers.

Q5: Can I book a guided float trip 
for the Black Caddis hatch?
A5: Yes — Mac Brown Fly Fish offers 
guided float trips and wade trips on 
the Tuckasegee River delayed harvest 
section in Bryson City, NC year-round. 
March and April float trips during the 
Black Caddis hatch are among the most 
exciting guided days of the entire year 
— fish are active on the surface, 
numbers are high, and the scenery of 
the Smoky Mountains in early spring 
is at its finest. All gear provided, 
all skill levels welcome.
