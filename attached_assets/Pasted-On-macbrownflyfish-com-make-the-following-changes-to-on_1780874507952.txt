FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: When is the best time for fall 
fly fishing in North Carolina?
A1: Mid-September through November 
is the prime fall fly fishing window 
in Western North Carolina. Water 
temperatures drop back into the 
productive 45-65°F range, the October 
Caddis hatch triggers aggressive 
surface feeding from large brown 
trout, and the delayed harvest 
sections reopen October 1 with 
fresh stockings on the Tuckasegee 
River. Fall scenery in the Smoky 
Mountains peaks in October — 
making it the most visually 
spectacular season of the year 
to be on the water.

Q2: What hatches occur during fall 
fly fishing in the Smokies?
A2: The October Caddis is the 
signature fall hatch in the Smoky 
Mountains — a large orange-bodied 
caddisfly that triggers some of the 
most aggressive dry fly takes of 
the entire year. Large parachute 
patterns, elk hair caddis, and 
stimulators in sizes 8-12 produce 
well during peak October Caddis 
activity. Midges and BWOs also 
become increasingly important as 
water temperatures cool through 
November into winter.

Q3: How does catch and release 
affect fall trout fishing in NC?
A3: Decades of catch and release 
on delayed harvest sections has 
created increasingly selective 
fish — trout that have been caught 
and released multiple times adapt 
rapidly and become harder to fool 
with standard patterns and poor 
presentations. This is a great 
thing for the resource and a 
genuine challenge for anglers. 
The delayed harvest sections 
produce some of the largest fish 
counts of any trout fishing in 
the eastern United States during 
fall — but reward anglers with 
strong presentation skills over 
those relying on fly pattern alone.

Q4: Where is the best fall fly 
fishing near Bryson City NC?
A4: The Tuckasegee River delayed 
harvest section through downtown 
Bryson City opens October 1 and 
provides excellent float and wade 
trip fishing through May. The 
Nantahala River tailwater fishes 
well year-round. The wild streams 
of Great Smoky Mountains National 
Park — over 2,900 miles of wild 
trout water — offer technical fall 
fishing for wild rainbow and brook 
trout with significantly less 
pressure than the delayed harvest 
sections.

Q5: Do international visitors 
fly fish the Smokies in fall?
A5: Yes — Mac Brown Fly Fish 
regularly guides international 
visitors during fall including 
anglers from Australia, New Zealand, 
Germany, Switzerland, Austria, 
Japan, England, and Scotland. 
Western North Carolina offers a 
combination of wild trout fishing, 
accessible delayed harvest water, 
and Smoky Mountain scenery that 
draws serious fly fishers from 
around the world — particularly 
in fall when conditions are at 
their finest. All gear provided, 
all skill levels welcome.
