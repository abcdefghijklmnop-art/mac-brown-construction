FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: What are the four stages of 
successful training in fly fishing?
A1: Stage 1 — Unconscious Incompetence: 
you don't know what you don't know. 
You think you're ready to fish after 
watching YouTube. Stage 2 — Conscious 
Incompetence: the light comes on and 
you realize how much there is to learn. 
This is the most important stage — 
humility opens the door to real 
improvement. Stage 3 — Conscious 
Competence: you know what to do but 
must think about it deliberately. 
Stage 4 — Unconscious Competence: 
the cast, the mend, the read — all 
happen instinctively in flow state.

Q2: How long does it take to reach 
unconscious competence in fly fishing?
A2: It varies widely — but the single 
biggest accelerator is quality 
instruction early. A fly fisher who 
receives certified casting instruction 
at the beginning builds correct 
fundamentals from day one and reaches 
conscious competence far faster than 
one who self-teaches and spends years 
reinforcing faults. Mac Brown's guided 
trips and instruction programs are 
designed to move anglers through 
stages two and three as efficiently 
as possible.

Q3: Why do most fly fishers stay 
stuck in stage two?
A3: Because stage two requires humility 
— and most fly fishers resist admitting 
what they don't know. The fly fishing 
industry makes it easier to buy a new 
rod than to take a lesson. More than 
60% of fly fishers fish fewer than a 
few days annually, never accumulating 
enough experience to naturally progress. 
The fastest path through stage two is 
a mentor or certified instructor who 
can identify faults and correct them 
before they become permanent habits.

Q4: What is unconscious incompetence 
in fly fishing?
A4: Unconscious incompetence is the 
starting point — when a fly fisher 
doesn't know what they don't know. 
They make casts that don't produce 
fish and blame the fly, the water, 
or the season rather than their 
presentation or mechanics. They've 
never been shown what correct casting, 
drift, or water reading looks like — 
so they have no frame of reference 
for what needs to improve. Every 
expert was once here. Recognizing 
it is the first step forward.

Q5: How does Mac Brown apply the 
four stages framework in instruction?
A5: Every guided trip and instructional 
program at Mac Brown Fly Fish in 
Bryson City, NC begins by identifying 
where the client is in the four stages 
— and tailoring the day accordingly. 
Stage two clients need fundamental 
corrections in casting and presentation. 
Stage three clients need refinement 
and challenge. Stage four clients 
benefit most from advanced clinics, 
competition preparation, and guide 
school. The Fly Fishing Guide School 
curriculum is built around accelerating 
students through the four stages in 
one concentrated week.
