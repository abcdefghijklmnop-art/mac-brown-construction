Why This Class Exists
Fly casting defines fly fishing. It is the only delivery between the angler and the fish — and yet it remains one of the most undertaught, oversimplified, and myth-laden skills in the sport.
Social media and retail-driven instruction have accelerated the problem. When casting fallacies are repeated often enough, they become accepted as fact — and getting back to truthful, proven fundamentals becomes an uphill battle. This class exists to fix that. Not with rigid rules and jargon, but with clarity, enthusiasm, and the kind of deep understanding that makes fly casting genuinely fun to learn and even more fun to teach.
Golfers visit a golf pro to establish proper fundamentals from the very beginning. Fly fishers deserve the same opportunity — and this weekend is it.

The Two Things That Will Blow Your Mind
Two concepts consistently produce the biggest light-bulb moments in this class — and they are the two things most lacking in fly casting instruction today:
Loop control. Most casters have a vague idea of what a good loop looks like. Very few understand exactly why it forms the way it does and how to shape it with intention. Once that becomes clear, everything else in fly casting starts to make sense in a new way.
Less is more. Loop efficiency is not about power — it is about the precise application of the right amount of force at exactly the right moment. When students feel this for the first time, the change in their casting is immediate and dramatic.
These are not advanced concepts reserved for competition casters. They are the fundamentals that everyone deserves to understand — and that this weekend delivers.

On Teaching
The certification is ultimately about teaching — and teaching is where the real joy lives.
The more you teach others, the more deeply you understand the material yourself. The art of becoming masterful at teaching any kinesthetic sport is keeping it simple, keeping it fun, and conveying fundamentals through analogies rather than rigid rules. Once you have gained confidence, you can start introducing family members, neighbors, and kids from your local community to the joys of fly fishing — and watch the sport grow one cast at a time.
Your community is where all meaningful change in fly casting eventually begins. Every great evolution in the sport — competitive angling, Spey casting, stillwater technique — started with a small group of committed people raising the standard locally. You can be that person. This weekend gets you there.
"The certification is about teaching in actuality. The process becomes so much easier with proper coaching because you actually understand the material — and that understanding is contagious."

Getting Ready — What to Bring
•	A 7-weight rod with a bright-colored fly line (Scientific Anglers Expert Distance is ideal)
•	A 7.5 to 8-foot leader
•	A notebook and pen — you will want to take notes
•	Download the FFI CI test in PDF format from the FFI website before you arrive — reading it in advance gives you a tremendous head start
•	A 100-foot tape measure and cones for practice sessions (recommended)
•	A relaxed grip and an open mind
Practice sessions before the class should be kept relatively short and genuinely enjoyable. You will quickly identify the areas where you are strong — and the areas where the weekend will unlock something new.
