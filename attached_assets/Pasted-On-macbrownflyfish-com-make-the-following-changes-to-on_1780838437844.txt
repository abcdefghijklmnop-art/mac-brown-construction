FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: Are all fly fishing tippet brands 
equal in performance?
A1: No — and Mac Brown's 1996 university 
tippet study proved it. Tested at the 
MSU and WCU materials labs, multiple 
brands showed significant differences 
in actual diameter versus labeled size, 
elasticity, abrasion resistance, and 
knot strength. Maxima Chameleon labeled 
as 4X measured closer to 2X at .009 
inches — significantly heavier than 
advertised. The study found that harder 
materials achieve greater abrasion 
resistance at the expense of breaking 
easier, while supple materials stretch 
further before failure.

Q2: What does tippet X size actually 
mean?
A2: The X system designates tippet 
diameter — subtract the X number from 
.011 inches to find the diameter. 4X 
equals .007 inches in diameter. However 
the study found that labeled X sizes 
frequently do not match actual measured 
diameters across brands. Two tippets 
labeled the same X size from different 
manufacturers can have significantly 
different actual diameters — which 
affects sink rate, visibility, knot 
strength, and breaking strength 
independently of the label.

Q3: What is the difference between 
monofilament and fluorocarbon tippet?
A3: Monofilament is supple, less expensive, 
and stretches more before breaking — 
making it more forgiving on hook sets 
and better for fishing in the film. 
Fluorocarbon is harder, denser, nearly 
invisible underwater, and sinks faster 
— making it superior for subsurface 
presentations and abrasion resistance. 
The study found that harder materials 
including fluorocarbon achieve greater 
abrasion resistance at the cost of 
lower stretch before failure. Mac Brown 
uses Maxima Chameleon for leaders and 
fluorocarbon tippet for subsurface 
fishing.

Q4: What tippet size should I use for 
trout fishing in the Smoky Mountains?
A4: Match tippet size to fly size and 
conditions. A rough guide: 3X for 
streamers and heavy nymphs, 4X-5X 
for standard nymph and wet fly rigs, 
6X for dry flies and small nymphs in 
clear water, 7X-8X for winter midge 
fishing and highly pressured fish. 
Water clarity, fish size, current 
speed, and fly air resistance all 
influence tippet selection. Always 
carry multiple sizes — changing 
tippet is the least expensive 
adjustment you can make.

Q5: Where can I learn more about 
leader and tippet construction?
A5: Mac Brown's leader construction 
guide at macbrownflyfish.com covers 
butt section design, taper formulas, 
material selection, and proven leader 
builds for dry fly, nymph, wet fly, 
and streamer fishing on Smoky Mountain 
trout streams — refined over 55+ years 
of guiding on the Tuckasegee, Nantahala, 
and wild streams of the Great Smoky 
Mountains. Mac Brown Fly Fish also 
offers private casting and rigging 
lessons in Bryson City, NC.
