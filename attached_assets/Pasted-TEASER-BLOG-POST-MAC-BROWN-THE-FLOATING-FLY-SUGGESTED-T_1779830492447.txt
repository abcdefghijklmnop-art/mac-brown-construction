SUGGESTED TITLE:
My Dry Fly Philosophy — Now Published in The Floating Fly

---

BODY:

I've spent decades on the water guiding, teaching, and quietly testing ideas that most folks never see written down. When The Floating Fly — one of the most respected fly fishing publications in the world — reached out and asked me to contribute, I figured it was time to put some of those thoughts on paper. The result is a piece that covers everything from equipment choices and leader design to reading water, entomology, and the five-component fish-fighting system we use on every guided trip.

A lot of what I wrote challenges conventional wisdom. Short rods, store-bought leaders, chasing bug identification — these are things the American fly fishing industry has long pushed, and they're often not what actually produces fish. The article lays out what 40+ years of guiding in the Southern Appalachians — and fishing everywhere from New Zealand to South America — has actually taught me.

Whether you're a beginner sorting out your first setup or a seasoned angler trying to break a plateau, I think there's something in here for you. Here's a small taste:

---

PULL QUOTES:

"One of the sayings we use during the schools is to 'fish the wrong flies well'… Spend more time perfecting the archer than looking for the perfect arrow."

"Leader length being too short is probably one of the biggest mistakes that I witness throughout the years instructing. A good starting point would be to begin with roughly 15 feet."

"Fly fishing for me is a triad approach of tactics, strategies, and line control."
