What You Will Learn
Distance Without Effort
Saltwater casting demands reach — and most anglers try to achieve it through brute force, which works against them every time. Learn the mechanics of generating line speed efficiently so longer casts feel easier than your current short ones. Distance is a byproduct of technique, not strength. Every participant leaves casting further than they arrived — guaranteed.
Accuracy Under Pressure
A cast that lands in the right zone at the right moment is worth ten beautiful casts that miss. Learn the presentation angles, timing, and target focus that produce accurate deliveries when everything is moving — the boat, the fish, and your heart rate.
Casting in Wind
Wind is the defining challenge of saltwater fly fishing — and the one thing most anglers never properly prepare for. Learn to cast with the wind, into the wind, across the wind, and in the swirling crosswinds that make flats fishing so demanding. Wind is not your enemy. Untrained casting in wind is. This workshop removes that obstacle completely.
The Saltwater Double Haul
The engine of every long, efficient saltwater cast. Most anglers have a version of a double haul. Very few have one that actually works under pressure at distance. Learn to haul with timing, efficiency, and purpose — and feel the difference immediately in your line speed and loop quality.
The Elliptical Cast — The Secret Weapon Most Anglers Never Learn
The elliptical cast is one of the most practical and underused tools in saltwater fly fishing — and one of the most immediately game-changing skills any caster can add to their arsenal. Where a standard cast travels in a single plane, the elliptical cast traces an oval path, combining the forward and backward stroke in a continuous, flowing motion that eliminates unnecessary pauses, reduces false casting, and keeps the fly moving toward the target with remarkable efficiency.
In saltwater, time is everything. A fish on the flats is moving constantly — and every false cast is a moment the window is closing. The elliptical cast solves this. It generates line speed and loop quality in fewer strokes, delivers the fly faster, and does so with a smoothness that reduces wind resistance and tailing loop risk simultaneously.
This cast is also a game-changer in tight quarters — when bankside vegetation, a low ceiling of mangroves, or a crowded boat deck limits the room available for a conventional back cast. The elliptical path keeps everything contained and controlled while still generating the power and accuracy the shot demands.
Once you feel an elliptical cast work for the first time — the way it loads the rod continuously and delivers energy to the fly line in one fluid arc — you will wonder how you ever fished without it.
"The elliptical cast feels like cheating once you learn it. Fewer false casts, more accuracy, less effort. It is one of the first things we teach in saltwater situations for exactly that reason."
Quick Release & Shooting Line
Getting the fly in the air fast and delivering it with minimal false casts is one of the most practical and underrated skills in saltwater fly fishing. A fish on the flats does not wait. Learn to be ready.
Sight Fishing Presentations
Dropping a fly at the right distance, on the right angle, without lining the fish or spooking it with a splashy presentation — this is the art within the art. Learn to control where your fly lands and how it enters the water, because presentation in saltwater is everything.
Leader Construction & Rigging
A saltwater leader that turns over correctly under load, handles the shock of a strip strike, and holds up to the abrasion of big fish is not an afterthought — it is a system. Learn the construction principles, knots, and rigging considerations that give your setup the best possible chance when a world-class fish is on the end.
The Strip Strike
The most common mistake freshwater anglers make in saltwater. Raising the rod to set the hook on a permit or tarpon costs more fish than any other single error. Learn the strip strike — why it works, when to use it, and how to make it instinctive before you ever step on a boat.
Casting to Moving Fish
Stationary targets are practice. Moving fish are the game. Learn to lead a fish correctly, adjust for speed and angle, and deliver the fly where the fish will be — not where it was. This single skill separates anglers who convert saltwater opportunities from those who watch them swim away.
Fly Selection & Strategy by Species
Bonefish, permit, tarpon, redfish, snook — every species demands a different approach, a different presentation, and a different set of expectations from the angler. Learn the strategic framework for approaching each pursuit so you arrive at your destination with a plan, not just hope.
