You're right — WordPress handled this automatically with featured images. In Next.js you have to set **Open Graph (OG) meta tags** manually. That's what Facebook, Instagram, X, etc. all read to generate the preview card with image, title, and description.

Here's exactly what you need.

## The Fix: Open Graph Meta Tags

Every page needs these tags in its `<head>`:

```html
<meta property="og:title" content="River Conditions & Hatch Chart — Bryson City NC Fly Fishing Report" />
<meta property="og:description" content="Current river flows, water temps, and what's hatching on the Tuckasegee and Nantahala." />
<meta property="og:image" content="https://macbrownflyfish.com/images/bryson-city-hatch-chart-og.jpg" />
<meta property="og:image:width" content="1200" />
<meta property="og:image:height" content="630" />
<meta property="og:url" content="https://macbrownflyfish.com/bryson-city-fly-fishing-report-hatch-chart" />
<meta property="og:type" content="article" />
```

## In Next.js App Router (your setup)

In each page's `page.tsx` or `page.js`, export a `metadata` object:

```js
export const metadata = {
  title: "River Conditions & Hatch Chart — Bryson City NC Fly Fishing Report",
  description: "Current river flows, water temps, and what's hatching on the Tuckasegee and Nantahala.",
  openGraph: {
    title: "River Conditions & Hatch Chart — Bryson City NC Fly Fishing Report",
    description: "Current river flows, water temps, and what's hatching on the Tuckasegee and Nantahala.",
    url: "https://macbrownflyfish.com/bryson-city-fly-fishing-report-hatch-chart",
    images: [
      {
        url: "https://macbrownflyfish.com/images/bryson-city-hatch-chart-og.jpg",
        width: 1200,
        height: 630,
        alt: "Bryson City NC fly fishing hatch chart and river report",
      },
    ],
    type: "article",
  },
  twitter: {
    card: "summary_large_image",
    title: "River Conditions & Hatch Chart — Bryson City NC",
    description: "Current flows, temps, and hatches on the Tuckasegee and Nantahala.",
    images: ["https://macbrownflyfish.com/images/bryson-city-hatch-chart-og.jpg"],
  },
};
```

## The OG Image Itself

The image is the critical piece Facebook actually shows. Requirements:

- **Size:** 1200 × 630px (required — Facebook will reject or crop anything else)
- **Format:** JPG preferred, under 1MB
- **Content:** A compelling photo of the river, a fish, or someone casting — not a logo on white
- **File location:** Put it in your `/public/images/` folder in Replit so it's served as a static asset

## For a Sitewide Default

In your root `layout.tsx`, set a fallback OG image that fires on any page that doesn't have its own:

```js
export const metadata = {
  metadataBase: new URL("https://macbrownflyfish.com"),
  openGraph: {
    images: ["/images/macbrownflyfish-og-default.jpg"],
  },
};
```

The `metadataBase` line is important — without it, Next.js won't convert relative image paths to full URLs, and Facebook won't find the image.

## After You Deploy

Test it at **developers.facebook.com/tools/debug** — paste the URL and it shows exactly what Facebook sees and lets you force a cache refresh. Do this every time you update an OG image.
