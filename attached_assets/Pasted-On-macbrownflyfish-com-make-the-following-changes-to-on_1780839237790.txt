FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: Does fly selection really matter 
in fly fishing?
A1: Less than most anglers think — and 
far less than the fly fishing industry 
would have you believe. Drift matters 
more than pattern in the vast majority 
of fishing situations. A well-presented 
attractor pattern with a drag-free drift 
consistently outfishes a precisely 
matched hatch fly with poor presentation. 
The exception is highly pressured, 
selective fish in slow clear water during 
a specific hatch — where size and profile 
matter more than exact imitation.

Q2: What matters more than fly 
selection in fly fishing?
A2: In order of importance — drift, 
approach and stealth, water reading 
and fish location, casting mechanics 
and line control, hook setting, and 
then fly selection. Most fly fishers 
invert this list entirely, spending 
their budget on flies and gear while 
neglecting the skills that actually 
produce fish. A creel study on the 
Upper Nantahala River found that 
only 2% of anglers catch over 90% 
of the trout — the top 2% are not 
using better flies.

Q3: Why does the fly fishing industry 
overemphasize fly selection?
A3: Because flies are easy to sell 
and skills are hard to sell. A new 
fly box is an immediate, tangible 
purchase. Better water reading and 
casting mechanics require time, 
practice, and ideally instruction. 
The industry's incentive is to 
convince anglers that the next fly, 
the next rod, or the next line will 
solve the problem — when the problem 
is almost always the angler's 
presentation, approach, or casting.

Q4: What is the most important 
skill to develop in fly fishing?
A4: Drift — achieving and maintaining 
a drag-free presentation. It is the 
foundation of dry fly fishing and 
nymph fishing alike. Without it no 
fly will consistently fool trout 
regardless of how well it matches 
the hatch. Drift is developed through 
better casting mechanics, longer 
leaders, upstream mending, proper 
approach angles, and water reading 
— all teachable skills that improve 
measurably with proper instruction.

Q5: How do I become a better fly 
fisher without buying more gear?
A5: Take a casting lesson. Book a 
guided wade trip focused on 
presentation rather than fish 
count. Practice mending on your 
home water. Study water reading — 
understanding where fish hold and 
why tells you more than any fly 
pattern ever will. Mac Brown Fly 
Fish in Bryson City, NC offers 
private casting lessons and 
instructional wade trips specifically 
focused on the skills that separate 
the top 2% of fly fishers from 
everyone else — led by the only 
FFI Master Casting Instructor in 
North Carolina since 1987.
