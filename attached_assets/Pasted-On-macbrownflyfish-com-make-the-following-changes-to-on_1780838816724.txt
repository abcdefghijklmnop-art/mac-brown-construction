consistently offered the greatest control

Q1: What is fly rod modulus?
A1: Modulus is a material's resistance to 
elastic deformation under load — in fly 
casting it refers to the rod's stiffness 
relative to its weight. Higher modulus 
graphite is stiffer and lighter for the 
same diameter blank. The evolution from 
fiberglass to graphite-3 to graphite-4 
has increased the stiffness-to-mass ratio 
with each generation — allowing rods to 
become lighter while maintaining similar 
stiffness. Higher modulus generally means 
smaller rod diameter and higher frequency.

Q2: What is fly rod dampening and why 
does it matter?
A2: Dampening is how quickly a fly rod 
returns to rest after being deflected — 
measured in seconds from maximum flex 
to stillness. A lower dampening number 
is better. All rods that rated highest 
in Mac Brown's WCU blind study also 
dampened most quickly. Poor dampening 
introduces oscillation into the cast 
after the stop, creating unwanted waves 
in the fly line that reduce accuracy 
and loop quality. Dampening data can 
also reveal continuity problems in 
rod taper when compared with frequency 
and deflection results.

Q3: What is rod spline and does it 
matter for fly casting?
A3: The spline is the stiffer side of 
the rod blank — ideally the guides 
should be aligned with the spine so 
the rod bends consistently in the 
same plane during casting. The WCU 
study found that only 34% of rods 
tested had guides splined exactly 
up or down. Rods with guides correctly 
aligned tracked straight during the 
dampening test — non-splined rods 
tracked in an elliptical pattern, 
indicating energy loss and reduced 
casting efficiency.

Q4: What fly rod action does Mac Brown 
recommend for trout fishing?
A4: Medium action — based on the WCU 
study findings. The panel of 20 
experienced casters with 470 combined 
years of experience consistently rated 
medium action rods highest for feel, 
control, and versatility across trout 
angling scenarios. Medium actions are 
easier for casting curves in either 
direction, allow the caster to feel 
the tip during the cast, and offer 
the greatest flexibility for the 
widest range of distances and 
presentation types. Avoid extreme 
fast actions unless distance casting 
is the primary goal.

Q5: How do I evaluate a fly rod 
before buying?
A5: Cast it at multiple distances — 
close, medium, and far — and pay 
attention to feel, not just loop shape. 
The rods that rated lowest in the WCU 
study lost their feel aspect at 
certain distances even though casters 
could compensate with technique. No 
caster would purchase a rod where the 
feel aspect was compromised regardless 
of performance data. Test dampening 
by flexing the tip and observing how 
quickly it settles. Balance the rod 
on a finger near the cork to evaluate 
swing weight. Then cast it on water 
before committing.
