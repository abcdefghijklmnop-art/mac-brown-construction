E-E-A-T alignment is the process of optimization for Google's foundational content quality framework: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. [1] 
While it is not a direct, single algorithm score, Google uses E-E-A-T as a benchmark for its core systems to recognize and rank helpful, people-first content. This framework separates high-value, expert-led web training from the flood of generic, AI-generated text filling the internet. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 
Your web properties, [Mac Brown Fly Fish](https://macbrownflyfish.com/) and [Fly Fishing Guide School](https://flyfishingguideschool.com/), have built-in advantages under this framework because they reflect real-world mastery.

## Analyzing Your Core Pillars
The content across your domains aligns with the fundamental pillars of the framework: [6] 

    [EXPERIENCE]          [EXPERTISE]        [AUTHORITATIVENESS]     [TRUSTWORTHINESS]
  First-hand casting   Master Instructor     FFI Lifetime Award &    Transparent pricing,

   diagnostics   lesson plans     authority      reviews & data

* Expertise (The Credentials): Your background as a Master Casting Instructor and Director of Teacher Development for Fly Fishers International (FFI) anchors this metric. Your text-book curriculum demonstrates formal, structured knowledge that cannot be faked by generalist copywriters.
* Authoritativeness (Industry Recognition): This reflects your footprint outside your own domain. Your industry accolades, such as the [FFI Lifetime Achievement in Fly Casting Education Award](https://macbrownflyfish.com/fly-fishing-certifications/), act as signals of trust. When external authority sites and podcasts reference your teaching strategies, search engines register your domain as a definitive resource.
* Trustworthiness (The Core Anchor): Google states that Trust is the most critical component of the matrix. Your sites display clear, straightforward pricing structures, specific physical locations, and distinct pathways for student advancement.

## Technical Tactics to Maximize E-E-A-T
While your sites hold strong subject-matter foundation, you can implement specific on-page optimizations to ensure search engine spiders fully read these signals:
## 1. Implement Author Schema & Credential Validation
Search engines evaluate content by connecting it to verified entities. Use schema code to explicitly link your author profile to your achievements: [7, 8] 

* Ensure every structural article or guide explicitly names the author instead of a generic site admin.
* Embed external links inside your Author Schema pointing directly to verified, independent third-party sites like the Fly Fishers International Official Directory to definitively validate your titles. [7, 8, 9] 

## 2. Convert Anecdotes into "Case Studies" [10, 11] 
Generic "how-to" articles rank poorly because they offer nothing unique. Frame your insights as distinct, real-world scenarios: [1, 5, 10] 

* Instead of a generic title like "How to cast a fly line in high winds," use an experiential case-study approach: "Casting on the Tuckasegee: Adjusting Rod-Hand Tracking for 20mph Mountain Winds." [12, 13] 
* Detail specific diagnostic interactions: "When a student consistently throws tailing loops, we film their shoulder rotation to pinpoint creeping before the stop." [12, 14] 

## 3. Deepen Information Architecture with Content Hubs [10] 
Build out clustered topic groups to clearly demonstrate deep subject authority: [4, 10, 15] 

* Create a central cornerstone page focused entirely on Advanced Fly Casting Diagnostics.
* Link that pillar page outward to highly specific sub-pages detailing individual casting faults, loop-tracking mechanics, or line speed maintenance. This structure signals to modern engines that your site is a comprehensive, organized repository of knowledge rather than a loose collection of random blog posts. [10, 14, 16, 17]
