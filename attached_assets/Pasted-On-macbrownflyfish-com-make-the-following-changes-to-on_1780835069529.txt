Q1: What are the most important fly fishing 
terms for beginners?
A1: Start with these five: Leader — the clear 
monofilament connecting fly line to fly. Tippet 
— the thin end of the leader where the fly 
attaches. Mend — repositioning fly line to 
achieve drag-free drift. Hatch — when large 
numbers of aquatic insects emerge simultaneously. 
Dead drift — when the fly moves at the same 
speed as the current, imitating a natural insect.

Q2: What is drag-free drift in fly fishing?
A2: Drag-free drift is when the fly moves at 
exactly the same speed as the current — 
imitating a natural insect. Achieving it requires 
proper mending, leader design, and presentation 
angle. It is the single most important skill in 
dry fly and nymph fishing and the most common 
reason fish refuse a fly.

Q3: What is matching the hatch?
A3: Matching the hatch means selecting an 
artificial fly that closely imitates the color, 
size, shape, and behavior of the natural insects 
fish are actively feeding on. When a hatch is 
occurring, trout become selective and often 
refuse flies that don't closely match the 
predominant species present.

Q4: What is the difference between a dry fly, 
nymph, and streamer?
A4: A dry fly is fished on the surface imitating 
adult aquatic insects. A nymph is fished 
subsurface imitating the immature stage — the 
most productive technique on most days since 
trout feed subsurface 80-90% of the time. A 
streamer imitates baitfish and is the most 
effective method for targeting the largest 
trout in any river system.

Q5: Where can I learn fly fishing in the
