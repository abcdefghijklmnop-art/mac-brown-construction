physics. Question everything before

over popular phrases every time.

Q2: What is the good enough mentality

A2: The good enough mentality describes 
the 95% of anglers who fish the same 
way their entire lives with little to 
no improvement — content with occasional 
fish, basic technique, and no drive to 
push further. The NCWRC creel study on 
the Upper Nantahala confirms it — the 
top 2.6% of anglers catch over a third 
of all fish landed. Moving beyond good 
enough requires establishing clear 
intent and goals, seeking mechanical 
instruction from certified professionals, 
practicing deliberately rather than 
repeatedly, and maintaining the humility 
to remain a student regardless of 
experience level.

Q3: What are fluffies in fly fishing?
A3: Fluffies is Mac Brown's term for 
self-promoting anglers and instructors 
who project authority without substance 
— teaching simplified principles from 
a shallow understanding while ranking 
themselves above peers who are living 
legends. The fluffy trend has accelerated 
with social media — hashtags, tweets, 
and follower counts substituting for 
genuine knowledge. Fluffies teach 
phrases instead of mechanics. The 
antidote is seeking instructors who 
can explain why a cast works — not 
just what to do — and who can connect 
every correction back to a verifiable 
mechanical principle.

Q4: What is intent in fly fishing 
instruction?
A4: Intent is the student's clearly 
defined goal before the lesson begins 
— what they want to achieve and why. 
Without established intent, the lesson 
drifts and the instructor cannot tailor 
the approach to what will produce the 
greatest improvement in the available 
time. Mac Brown's approach to every 
lesson begins with understanding the 
student's intent — whether the goal 
is catching more fish, passing FFI 
certification, improving distance, or 
developing specific presentation casts. 
Intent determines everything that 
follows.

Q5: How do I find a fly fishing 
instructor who teaches mechanical 
truth?
A5: Look for FFI certified casting 
instructors — particularly Certified 
Casting Instructors and Master Casting 
Instructors — who can explain why a 
cast works in plain mechanical terms. 
An instructor teaching mechanical truth
