FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: What causes drag in fly fishing?
A1: Drag occurs when fly line, leader, or 
tippet sits in a current that moves at a 
different speed than the current carrying 
the fly — the faster current pulls the 
line and drags the fly unnaturally. Major 
drag is obvious — the fly skates rapidly 
across the surface. Micro drag is subtle 
— the fly appears to float naturally but 
is under slight tension that trout detect 
and refuse. Both result in refusals. The 
solution is mending — repositioning the 
fly line upstream of the fly immediately 
after the cast lands.

Q2: How do I mend fly line to get a 
drag-free drift?
A2: Mend immediately after the cast 
lands — before the current has a chance 
to load tension into the line. Flip the 
rod tip upstream to reposition the fly 
line above the fly without moving the 
fly itself. In complex currents with 
multiple speed lanes between you and 
the fish, stack mends — multiple small 
upstream flips throughout the drift. 
The goal is to keep the fly line 
consistently upstream of the fly for 
the entire drift length.

Q3: How long should my leader be 
for drag-free drift?
A3: Much longer than most anglers use. 
Store-bought 7 and 9-foot leaders are 
inadequate for achieving serious drag-
free drift at distance — they require 
significantly more line control and 
produce shorter effective drifts. Mac 
Brown uses leaders of 12-30 feet 
depending on conditions. A longer 
leader keeps more fly line off the 
conflicting currents between the 
angler and the fish — dramatically 
increasing effective drift length 
with less effort.

Q4: Does drag affect nymph fishing 
as well as dry fly fishing?
A4: Yes — drag affects every method 
including wet flies, nymphs, and 
streamers when a dead drift is the 
goal. Micro drag on a nymph is 
harder to detect than on a dry fly 
but equally damaging to the 
presentation. Strike indicators 
help detect subtle drag — if the 
indicator moves faster or slower 
than the surrounding surface 
currents, the nymph is dragging. 
Mend immediately when this occurs.

Q5: What is the fastest way to 
improve my drag-free drift?
A5: Two things — lengthen your 
leader and improve your water 
reading. A longer leader immediately 
reduces the amount of line in 
conflicting currents. Better water 
reading tells you which currents 
will load tension into the line 
before it happens — allowing you 
to mend proactively rather than 
reactively. Mac Brown Fly Fish 
offers private wade trips in 
Bryson City, NC specifically 
focused on presentation — the 
fastest way to develop drag-free 
drift in real fishing conditions.
