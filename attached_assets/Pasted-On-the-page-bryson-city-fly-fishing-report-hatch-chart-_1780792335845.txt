Q1: What insects are hatching in the Smoky 
Mountains right now?
A1: Water temperature is the most reliable 
trigger for hatch activity. Below 45°F expect 
midges and small Blue Winged Olives. From 
45-55°F Quill Gordons, Blue Quills, and Black 
Stones begin. From 55-65°F the most diverse 
and prolific hatches of the year occur. Above 
65°F sulphurs, cahills, Isonychia, and 
terrestrials dominate. Check the automated 
conditions widget above for current water 
temperatures on the Tuckasegee and Nantahala.

Q2: What is the best fly for trout fishing 
in the Smoky Mountains?
A2: Caddisflies are the most abundant insect 
family in the Smoky Mountains region — over 
330 species identified in the surrounding 
watershed. Carry larval, pupal, and adult 
caddis patterns year-round in a range of 
sizes and colors. Patterns with hot spots, 
colored triggers, and rubber legs consistently 
outperform traditional drab patterns on these 
streams. In summer, never leave home without 
ants, beetles, and small hoppers.

Q3: What is the best water temperature for 
trout fishing in the Smokies?
A3: Mid-50s Fahrenheit is the ideal water 
temperature for trout fishing in the Smoky 
Mountains. At 55-65°F trout are most active 
and the best hatches of the year are 
occurring. Stop fishing for trout when water 
temperatures reach 65°F — switch to warm 
water species or target higher elevation 
streams where temperatures remain cooler.

Q4: What CFS is good for fly fishing the 
Tuckasegee River?
A4: The Tuckasegee River fishes well on float 
trips from 500 to 4,000 CFS. Below 500 CFS 
focus on the freestone streams in Great Smoky 
Mountains National Park instead. Above 4,000 
CFS float trips are off — bank fishing only 
from safe access points. The automated 
conditions widget above shows current CFS 
updated every 15 minutes.

Q5: When is the best time to fly fish the 
Nantahala River?
A5: The Nantahala fishes best when TVA 
generation is off — typically until noon. 
Wading is excellent during off-generation 
periods but always check the TVA release 
schedule before wading and stay on the 
road side of the river for safe exit when 
generation resumes. Float trips are 
recommended when generation is running. 
Check the automated conditions widget 
above for current Nantahala CFS.
