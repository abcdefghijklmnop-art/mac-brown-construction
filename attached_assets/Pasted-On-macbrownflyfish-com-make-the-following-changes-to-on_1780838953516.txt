FAQ — add before footer as expandable accordion:

Q1: How do I choose a fly fishing guide 
in North Carolina?
A1: Look for guides who hold FFI casting 
instructor certification, ACA whitewater 
rowing training, and first aid 
qualifications — these credentials 
indicate a guide who takes the profession 
seriously enough to invest in it. 
Independent guides who own their 
businesses outright consistently 
outperform fly shop guides because no 
one works harder for quality when their 
livelihood depends on it. Read reviews 
carefully — look for mentions of 
instruction, learning, and improvement, 
not just fish counts.

Q2: What is the difference between a 
guided fly fishing trip and a fly 
shop trip?
A2: Most brick-and-mortar fly shop 
trips assign less experienced guides 
who spend the day lobbing indicator 
rigs with minimal instruction or 
advice. Independent guides with 
decades of home water experience 
and professional certifications 
deliver a fundamentally different 
experience — one built around your 
goals on the water, your casting 
mechanics, and leaving you with 
skills you can apply on your own. 
You get what you pay for is true 
in fly fishing guiding.

Q3: What should I expect from a 
guided fly fishing trip?
A3: A quality guided fly fishing 
trip is instructional at every step 
— casting, water reading, rigging, 
presentation, hook setting, and 
fish playing. You should leave 
understanding why you caught fish 
and how to replicate it on your 
own. If a guide is simply netting 
fish for you without teaching the 
how and why behind every decision, 
you are on the wrong trip. The 
best guided days change how you 
fish permanently.

Q4: How much does a fly fishing 
guide cost in North Carolina?
A4: Guided float trips and wade 
trips at Mac Brown Fly Fish run 
$700 for a half day and $800 for 
a full day — 1 or 2 anglers at 
the same rate. Lake trips are 
$875 for a full day. Beware of 
heavily discounted trips — the 
economics of guiding mean that 
a guide offering significantly 
below-market rates is cutting 
corners somewhere, whether in 
experience, equipment, or time 
on the water.

Q5: What makes Mac Brown Fly Fish 
different from other guide services 
in NC?
A5: Mac Brown Fly Fish is the 
original fly fishing guide service 
in Western North Carolina — 
operating since 1987, led by the 
only FFI Master Casting Instructor 
in the state, with a faculty that 
includes 4 Master Casting 
Instructors, 5 ACA certified 
whitewater rowing instructors, 
3 Fly Fishing Team USA members, 
and 2 Hall of Fame inductees. 
Every trip is instructional. 
Every guide is credentialed. 
No one on staff is there just 
for the lifestyle.
