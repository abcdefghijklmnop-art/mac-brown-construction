Q1: When should you stop fly fishing 
for trout in the Smoky Mountains?
A1: Stop fishing for trout when water 
temperatures reach 65°F — and 
absolutely stop at 70°F. Trout caught 
in 70°F+ water have a very slim chance 
of survival even when carefully released. 
July and August are the off-season for 
trout fishing in Western North Carolina. 
Guides and outfitters who book summer 
trout trips regardless of water 
temperature are profiteers — not stewards 
of the resource. Check the live water 
temperature widget at macbrownflyfish.com 
before heading out.

Q2: What should fly fishers target 
in summer when trout streams are 
too warm?
A2: Smallmouth bass, carp, perch, and 
bluegill in the warmer river systems 
of Western North Carolina — the Little 
Tennessee River in particular. These 
species thrive in summer water 
temperatures and provide excellent 
fly fishing action with similar 
techniques to trout fishing. Chub on 
the wild streams of Great Smoky 
Mountains National Park also provide 
a great opportunity to practice 
nymphing techniques through the 
middle of the day. Mac Brown offers 
warm water guided trips during summer.

Q3: How do I choose an ethical 
fly fishing guide in North Carolina?
A3: Ask the guide directly: do you 
fish for trout when water temperatures 
exceed 65°F? Any guide who answers 
yes — or who books summer trout trips 
without checking water temperatures 
— is not a steward of the resource. 
Also ask about their qualifications, 
teaching ability, and whether the 
trip includes genuine instruction. 
Price alone is not a reliable indicator 
— guides in the Smoky Mountains range 
from $40 to over $500 for the same 
water. Look for certified instructors 
with verifiable credentials.

Q4: Is excessive fishing pressure 
damaging trout streams in the 
Smoky Mountains?
A4: Yes — and it is an increasing 
problem on delayed harvest streams 
due to the growing number of new 
fly fishers, guides, and outfitters. 
A delayed harvest stream requires 
only 30 feet of space between 
anglers — but the sheer volume of 
pressure concentrates fish and makes 
them increasingly selective over 
time. Wild streams require a mile 
or more of separation for a quality 
experience. Learn to read secondary 
water and fish away from crowds.

Q5: Why does Mac Brown not fish 
for trout in July and August?
A5: Because it is the ethical 
decision for the resource. Trout 
caught in water above 70°F face 
severely elevated mortality even 
with careful catch and release. 
Mac Brown Fly Fish switches to 
warm water species during the 
height of summer — targeting 
smallmouth bass and carp — and 
returns to trout guiding when 
water temperatures drop back into 
the productive range in September. 
This is not a business decision. 
It is a conservation decision.
