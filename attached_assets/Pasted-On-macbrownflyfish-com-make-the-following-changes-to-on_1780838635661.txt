Q1: What is a switch rod in fly fishing?
A1: A switch rod is a mid-length two-handed 
fly rod — typically 10 to 12 feet — designed 
to be cast with both single-hand and two-
handed techniques. It bridges the gap between 
a standard single-hand rod and a full Spey 
rod, offering the reach and line control of 
a two-handed rod with the versatility to 
fish tight quarters where a full Spey rod 
is too long. Switch rods excel on large 
rivers for streamers, wet fly swings, 
nymphing at distance, and mending at ranges 
a single-hand rod cannot manage.

Q2: What are the advantages of a switch 
rod for trout fishing?
A2: The primary advantage is comfort — 
distributing the casting load between two 
hands dramatically reduces arm and shoulder 
fatigue on long days on the water. Switch 
rods allow roll casts out 100+ feet to 
cover water that is too fast or deep to 
wade. They make mending at distance 
significantly easier, improve line control 
for dead drift nymphing at range, and 
allow active streamer presentations across 
water that single-hand rods cannot reach. 
Mac Brown particularly recommends them 
for youth and older anglers.

Q3: What fly line works best for a 
switch rod?
A3: Line selection is more important than 
rod selection for switch rod fishing. Mac 
Brown uses a 450-grain head of approximately 
45 feet — heavy enough to load the rod 
efficiently for both roll casts and aerial 
casts. Skagit heads work well for heavy 
streamers and sinking presentations. 
Scandinavian or Scandi heads work better 
for lighter wet flies and dry fly 
presentations. The line drives performance 
far more than the rod.

Q4: Can you nymph fish with a switch rod?
A4: Yes — and the switch rod is exceptional 
for nymphing at distance. You can attach 
an indicator and fish dead drift at ranges 
a single-hand rod cannot control effectively. 
The additional length provides superior 
mending ability and keeps more line off 
the water for cleaner drifts in complex 
currents. Mac Brown uses the switch rod 
for targeting rising fish with dry flies 
during BWO hatches on the Tuckasegee 
and Nantahala in the afternoons.

Q5: Where can I learn to cast a switch 
rod in the Smoky Mountains?
A5: Mac Brown Fly Fish in Bryson City, NC 
offers the Spey Elite Casting Clinic — a

two-handed casting techniques. All two-

by Mac Brown, the only FFI Master Casting 
Instructor in North Carolina, teaching 
switch rod casting since the early 1990s.
