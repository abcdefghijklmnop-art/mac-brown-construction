The short answer is **yes, absolutely**—but how you should do it depends on how many pages you changed and how quickly you want search engines to notice.

When you make major updates across lots of pages, notifying search engines helps them prioritize recrawling your site to reflect those changes (and your updated content) in search results.

Here is the best way to handle it:

## 1. Update the `<lastmod>` Tag First

Before doing anything else, ensure your sitemap file actually reflects the changes. Your website framework or SEO plugin should update the `<lastmod>` (last modified) timestamp for every page you changed.

Search engines read this tag to see which pages have fresh content without having to re-download the entire page first.

## 2. Choose Your Notification Method

Depending on your setup, choose one of the following methods to let search engines know:

* **The Best Method: Submit via Search Consoles**
Log into **Google Search Console** (and Bing Webmaster Tools), go to the **Sitemaps** section, and hit submit. This forces a direct ping to their systems and gives you a clear status report if there are any processing errors.
* **The Hands-Off Method: Automatic XML Updates**
If you are using a modern framework or an SEO plugin that automatically regenerates your sitemap, search engines will eventually find the updates on their own during their routine crawls. However, manually pushing it via Search Console speeds up the process significantly for large batches of updates.

---

## What About Single, High-Priority Pages?

If you have a handful of updated pages that are absolutely critical for your traffic or business conversions, don't wait for the sitemap to crawl.

Go to the top search bar in **Google Search Console**, paste the specific URL, and click **Request Indexing**. This puts those specific pages into a priority queue to be crawled much faster than a standard sitemap update.
