What You Will Learn
River Reading — The Foundation of Everything
The river is constantly communicating. Eddies, seams, hydraulics, pillows, holes, and tongues — every feature is a sentence in a language that, once learned, makes the water feel like a map rather than a mystery. River reading is the single most important skill a rower can develop, and it is the skill that takes longest to develop alone. Two days of guided instruction accelerates that understanding dramatically — combining classroom discussion with on-the-water application so that every concept clicks in real time, on real water.
Whitewater Classification & Grading Systems
Understanding the international whitewater grading system — Class I through Class VI — gives every river runner a universal language for assessing conditions, communicating with other paddlers, and making smart decisions about which water is right for their current skill level. Know what you are getting into before you get into it.
Boat & River Terminology
River running has its own vocabulary, and knowing it matters. From river left and river right to ferry angles, pivot points, and the anatomy of a hydraulic — this shared language makes instruction clearer, communication faster, and decision-making on the water more precise. You will leave speaking the language of the river fluently.
Craft Maneuverability & Control
This is where the oars become an extension of your intention. Learn the fundamental strokes — the draw, the push, the pry, the ferry — and understand not just how to execute each one but when and why to reach for it. Boat control is the difference between going where the river takes you and going where you decide to go. This academy builds the latter.
Stroke Diagnostics — Fix What Isn't Working
One of the most valuable components of the academy is the diagnostic work. Every rower develops habits — some good, some not. Having experienced, certified eyes watching your strokes and providing immediate, specific feedback is something you simply cannot get from a book, a video, or solo time on the water. Most inefficiencies are corrected quickly once they are identified clearly. Leave without the habits that would have taken years to discover and fix on your own.
Boat Rigging & Weight Placement
A properly rigged boat is a safer, more responsive, and more enjoyable boat. Learn how to set up your oar frame correctly, how to distribute weight for optimal trim and maneuverability, how to secure gear for moving water, and how to outfit your craft for the specific type of river running you love most — whether that is fishing, exploration, or overnight adventure. The setup decisions you make before you launch affect every moment on the water. Make them with knowledge and intention.
Safety Fundamentals
Confidence on the river is built on a foundation of safety awareness, not recklessness. Learn river rescue fundamentals, swimmer protocols, strainer and hazard recognition, and the decision-making frameworks that keep every river trip a great story rather than a cautionary one. Safety is not about fear — it is about preparation. Prepared rowers are confident rowers. Confident rowers have more fun.
Reading & Running Lines
Once you can read the water and control the boat, the next step is putting them together — identifying the best line through a rapid and executing it with intention. Learn to scout effectively, commit to a line confidently, and adjust in real time when the river does what rivers always do: surprise you. Running a clean line through water that once seemed chaotic is one of the most satisfying experiences the outdoors has to offer.
Gear, Equipment & Outfitting
What do you actually need on the river — and what is marketing noise? Learn the essential gear for safe and enjoyable river running, how to evaluate and select the right equipment for your style of rowing, and how to care for and maintain what you have. Whether you are outfitting a drift boat for fly fishing or a raft for expedition travel, the principles are the same and the knowledge is invaluable.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need my own boat or equipment?
No. All necessary equipment is provided for the academy. Contact us if you have questions about bringing your own gear.
What physical fitness level is required?
Rowing requires moderate physical effort, but this academy is designed to be accessible to a wide range of fitness levels. If you have specific concerns, reach out before registering.
Is this appropriate for complete beginners?
Absolutely. The academy is specifically designed to build a proper foundation from the ground up. No prior experience is necessary or assumed.
What type of craft will we be rowing?
Oar frame drift boats — the same platform used for guided fly fishing float trips throughout the region. The skills translate directly to any oar-powered craft.
Can this academy help me become a better fishing guide?
Yes. Strong boat control is one of the most important skills a fishing guide can have — and one of the most commonly underdeveloped. This academy addresses it directly and comprehensively.
Will we be on the water both days?
Yes. Both days combine on-water instruction with focused discussion and diagnostic work. Expect to spend the majority of your time where the learning happens best — on the river.
