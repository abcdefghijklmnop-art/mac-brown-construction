Q1: What is the Fly Fisher's Triad 
of strategies, tactics, and mechanics?
A1: The Fly Fisher's Triad is Mac Brown's 
framework for understanding fly fishing 
development — three equally important 
components that work together on every 
outing. Mechanics involve being fluent 
with what is possible through casting — 
the easiest to teach and learn. Tactics 
include how you control the flies in 
terms of action — dead drift, swing, 
jig, induced strike — taught quickly 
on the stream. Strategies involve 
recognizing insect activity, water 
types, and fish behavior — the hardest 
to develop and the longest to master. 
All three must work together for 
consistently successful fly fishing.

Q2: What is the Delayed Harvest 
program in North Carolina and who 
started it?
A2: The North Carolina Delayed Harvest 
program is a catch-and-release stocking 
program that runs October 1 through 
May 31 on designated streams — including 
the Tuckasegee River through downtown 
Bryson City. Bobby Setzer of the NC 
Wildlife Resources Commission was 
instrumental in the implementation 
of the Delayed Harvest concept — 
creating one of the most accessible 
and productive trout fishing programs 
in the southeastern United States. 
Mac Brown has been guiding on the 
Tuckasegee Delayed Harvest section 
since its inception.

Q3: How has Mac Brown been involved 
in fly fishing conservation and 
community programs?
A3: Mac Brown has coached Team NC and 
Team USA Youth for competition-style 
angling, hosted champion anglers from 
abroad for loch-style clinics in Bryson 
City, and been actively involved in 
Project Healing Waters for veterans 
and Casting for Recovery for cancer 
survivors. He has also worked with 
Boy Scouts of America and taught fly 
fishing classes at Western Carolina 
University — where many students 
became lifelong friends and advocates 
for the sport and conservation.

Q4: What organizations protect 
fly fishing water and community 
access?
A4: Fly Fishers International and 
Trout Unlimited are the two most 
important organizations for protecting 
fly fishing water, advocating for 
clean water policy, and preserving 
access for future generations. Both 
organizations provide community with 
others who share the same passion for 
clean water and wild places. Mac Brown 
has been actively involved in FFI 
throughout his career — serving on 
the Casting Board of Governors, as 
Associate Editor of The Loop Journal, 
and as Director of Teacher Development 
for FFI Casting Instructor Education.

Q5: How do I get started in fly 
fishing in Bryson City NC?
A5: Mac Brown Fly Fish in Bryson City, 
NC offers beginner fly fishing lessons, 
guided trips, and weekend schools — 
all gear provided, all skill levels 
welcome. The Tuckasegee River Delayed 
Harvest section flows right through 
downtown Bryson City and provides 
some of the most accessible and 
productive trout fishing in Western 
North Carolina. Bryson City is the 
trout mecca of the Smoky Mountains — 
Swain County is over 85% government

National Park.
